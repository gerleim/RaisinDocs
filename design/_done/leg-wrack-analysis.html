Certainly. Here's a structured summary.

RAW (Chronicles of Darkness)

One Leg Wrack

Effects:

  • Speed is halved (minimum 1).
  • Defense is reduced by 2.
  • Physical rolls that require movement are reduced to a chance die.

Both Legs Wrack

Additional effects:

  • The character takes the Knocked Down Tilt.
  • The character cannot stand up until the Tilt is removed.
  • Speed becomes 1.
  • If the character wants to move at all, they cannot take any other action that turn.
  • Physical rolls that require movement are reduced to a chance die.

Knocked Down Tilt

Relevant effects:

  • The character is prone.
  • If the character has not yet acted this turn, they lose their action.
  • They retain their Defense.
  • Attacks while prone suffer a –2 penalty.
  • Normally the Tilt ends by standing up, but Leg Wrack explicitly forbids standing up.

The Problem

Several rules overlap without explaining how they interact.

Question 1 — What counts as a "Physical roll requiring movement"?

The rules never define this.

Examples:

  • running ✔
  • climbing ✔
  • jumping ✔
  • Dodge?
  • melee attacks?
  • grappling?
  • charging?

No definition exists.

Question 2 — Melee attacks

Suppose the target is already adjacent.

RAW gives:

  • prone attack: –2
  • movement rolls: chance die

Is swinging a sword while lying on the ground

  • simply an attack (–2), or
  • a Physical roll requiring movement (chance die)?

RAW never answers.

Question 3 — Dodge

Dodge is a contested Physical roll.

It clearly involves movement.

RAW never states whether Dodge becomes a chance die.

Earlier we discussed that a strict reading suggests:

  • passive Defense → –2
  • active Dodge → chance die

…but this is an inference.

Question 4 — Moving then attacking

Both Legs says:

If you move at all, you cannot take any other action.

So:

crawl 1 yard → attack?

RAW says no.

Overall

No accepted community interpretation appears to exist.

My Conclusions

I think the RAW is internally inconsistent or at least under-specified.

The designers appear to have layered effects without considering every interaction.

They combined:

  • Speed reduction
  • Defense penalty
  • chance-die movement
  • prone
  • cannot stand
  • movement consumes action

but never defined how these interact with combat rolls.

A conservative RAW reading

I would interpret it as:

Movement actions

  • chance die

Running

  • chance die

Jumping

  • chance die

Climbing

  • chance die

Crawling

  • chance die

Melee attack without moving

  • normal attack
  • –2 if prone

Move then attack

  • impossible (movement uses your action)

Passive Defense

  • –2

Active Dodge

  • probably chance die (requires movement), but this is an interpretation rather than an explicit rule.

What I would house-rule

I would simplify the Tilt considerably.

One Leg

  • Half Speed.
  • –2 Defense.

Both Legs

  • Knocked Down.
  • Cannot stand.
  • Speed 1.
  • Moving uses your action.

I would remove the sentence

"Physical rolls that require movement are reduced to a chance die."

or interpret it as applying only to explicit movement actions (running, climbing, jumping, balancing, crawling, etc.), not to attacks or Dodge.

I think this better matches the apparent design intent:

  • one leg = you're hobbled,
  • both legs = you're effectively immobilized,

without creating ambiguous edge cases about every combat roll involving bodily movement.

Overall, I don't think there's enough evidence to claim a single "correct" RAW interpretation beyond the text itself. The lack of community consensus and official clarification suggests that most Storytellers simply adjudicate these interactions based on the situation rather than treating them as settled rules.
